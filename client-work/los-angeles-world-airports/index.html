Los Angeles World Airports

Pro-Bono Strategy ConsultantAccessibility strategy and implementation

Developed an accessibility strategy for a major airport modernization effort. My work focused on risks, mitigations, and phased implementation.

Project
Overview

Los Angeles World Airports engaged our team during a period of major change. We needed to make a new passenger journey understandable, accessible, and operationally ready. We looked at the full airport system. Our work connected communication, digital tools, physical environments, staff readiness, and long-term measurement. We treated accessibility as part of the full passenger experience.

A note on confidentialityThis case study omits confidential data, client findings, financial estimates, and operational details. It focuses on our process, rationale, and recommendations.

01 / Project Context

Project Context

An airport journey includes many transitions. Passengers plan their trip. They arrive on airport property. They find transportation, follow directions, and ask for help. Different organizations own different parts of this experience.

Modernization makes these transitions more difficult. A technical change can work as intended and still confuse passengers. People may not know what changed. They may struggle to find information. They may also struggle to find help. These challenges affect travelers with disabilities, first-time visitors, multilingual travelers, and passengers under time pressure.

Our work went beyond one service. We studied the modernization program, clarified messaging and marketing tactics, and identified ways to track future success. Accessibility became part of the operating model from the start.

02 / Research Approach

Research Approach

We focused on three questions. What information does a passenger need before arriving? What support does the passenger need at the airport? What does the airport need to deliver a consistent experience?

01

Communication

Clarify the message. Identify the right timing and channels.

02

Passenger Support

Connect digital tools, physical touchpoints, and human support.

03

Measurement

Define clear indicators for progress and improvement.

Our research included a review of the existing passenger journey. We studied similar transportation systems and major event environments. We also reviewed accessibility practices across communication, infrastructure, and service delivery. We evaluated each idea based on passenger value, feasibility, and operational reliability.

03 / Recommended Strategy

Recommended Strategy

We designed one coordinated system. We did not want a list of disconnected accessibility features. Communication, digital tools, staff support, and the physical environment needed to reinforce one another. Each improvement also needed a clear owner.

Communication

Communication Strategy

Use clear and consistent messages before and during travel.

Digital

Digital Tools

Give travelers timely guidance. Use digital tools alongside human assistance.

People

Staff Training

Provide shared references, clear escalation paths, and practical training.

Environment

Physical Environment

Align physical touchpoints with the same information used across other channels.

04 / Risks and Mitigations

Risks and Mitigations

My primary focus was risks, mitigations, and implementation. I used risk analysis to test each recommendation. The strategy needed to work across handoffs, operational constraints, and unexpected airport conditions.

Risk 01

Fragmented Responsibility

Why it matters Passengers experience one journey. Multiple teams may own different parts of it.

Mitigation Establish clear roles, shared decisions, handoff protocols, and one coordination platform.

Risk 02

Uneven Readiness

Why it matters New tools fail without consistent guidance.

Mitigation Use modular training, shared references, scenario practice, and regular feedback.

Risk 03

Cost and Complexity

Why it matters Complex solutions can be difficult to scale and maintain.

Mitigation Start with high-impact, low-cost initiatives. Pilot each solution before scaling. Define long-term ownership.

Risk 04

Operational Disruption

Why it matters Irregular operations can make one channel unreliable.

Mitigation Build escalation and backup pathways. Use multiple communication channels. Define crisis coordination processes.

This process made the strategy more specific. Every recommendation needed an owner and clear dependencies. It also needed a way to detect failure and respond to change.

05 / Prioritization

Prioritization Framework

A long list of ideas is not an implementation plan. We organized each opportunity by impact and feasibility. This helped the client separate urgent improvements from long-term investments.

◎

◇

Long-Term Investments

↺

Lower-priority ideas for future evaluation.

The framework was designed to change over time. New technology could change feasibility. Pilot results could also clarify the value of an idea.

06 / Implementation

Implementation Plan

The plan connected each recommendation to the capabilities needed for delivery. We did not launch every idea at once. The sequence created measurable progress and space to learn.

01

Alignment

Confirm ownership, decision rights, dependencies, success criteria, and team coordination.

02

Pilot Program

Test selected experiences. Collect feedback and resolve operational gaps.

03

Expansion

Scale successful ideas with consistent training, communication, documentation, and support.

04

Continuous Improvement

Monitor performance. Respond to change and keep the system current.

This phased model also supported high-volume events. It separated immediate needs from long-term improvements. Some solutions could mature through pilots and infrastructure planning.

07 / Measurement

Performance Measurement

We identified four measurement areas: passenger satisfaction, pilot evaluation, infrastructure, and staff training. These areas covered both passenger outcomes and organizational readiness.

Experience

Passenger Satisfaction

Measure clarity, confidence, ease of navigation, and access to support.

Evidence

Pilot Evaluation

Compare expected behavior with actual use. Collect feedback before scaling.

Reliability

Infrastructure Performance

Track the accuracy, availability, and maintenance of physical and digital tools.

Capability

Staff Readiness

Assess consistency, escalation confidence, and the usefulness of shared resources.

The goal was to create a useful feedback loop. The results supported real-time coordination and long-term planning. They also showed progress and informed future decisions.

08 / Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

Risk Analysis

Risk analysis improved the recommendation. It exposed unclear handoffs, untested assumptions, and single points of failure.

Implementation Planning

Implementation planning changed the final strategy. We considered ownership, timing, training, dependencies, and measurement. This moved the work from an ideal concept to a practical plan.

The project changed how I think about accessibility. It cannot be added at the end. It must shape communication, team coordination, physical design, and measurement from the start.
