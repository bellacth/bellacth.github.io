Omni Hotels & Resorts

Pro-Bono Strategy ConsultantFood & beverage growth and implementation

Built a scalable strategy for restaurant growth and guest engagement. The strategy also supported consistent execution across a diverse hotel portfolio.

Project
Overview

Omni Hotels & Resorts asked our team to explore its food and beverage operations. The goal was to build a stronger and more self-sustaining business. We studied restaurant economics, data quality, pricing, discounts, staff practices, technology, and customer capture. The final strategy needed to scale across properties. It also needed to preserve the character of each location.

A note on confidentialityThis case study omits confidential data, property findings, financial estimates, and operational details. It focuses on our process, rationale, and recommendations.

01 / Business Challenge

Business Challenge

Hotel restaurants serve two purposes. They support the hotel guest experience. They also operate as standalone businesses. Our strategy needed to improve individual restaurants and create systems for the full portfolio.

Omni needed consistent pricing, stronger efficiency, and scalable revenue opportunities. Each property also needed to preserve its local identity.

We focused on three outcomes. Increase the value of each visit. Give hotel guests and local diners reasons to return. Build the operational foundation for repeatable improvement.

02 / Research

Research Approach

Our research combined operating analysis, market research, and expert interviews. We focused on ideas with clear implementation paths. We also tested their ability to scale.

01

Operational Analysis

Review menu performance, server practices, discounts, and restaurant systems.

02

Marketing Analysis

Review marketing channels, messaging, and customer activations.

03

Expert Interviews

Interview operators, hospitality academics, and brand leaders.

We studied the guest journey and the operating model together. A promotion can increase traffic and still reduce value. Clear pricing rules matter. A strong menu can also underperform without staff support. New technology cannot solve inconsistent data.

03 / Diagnosis

Problem Diagnosis

Our diagnosis identified four gaps. Restaurant data and naming conventions were inconsistent. Pricing and discount decisions lacked a shared governance process. Training practices varied across locations. Marketing and loyalty opportunities lacked a reliable operating foundation.

Foundation 01

Data Consistency

Question Do teams use the same names, categories, and data standards?

Rationale National analysis requires accurate and consistent data.

Foundation 02

Pricing and Discounts

Question Do pricing changes follow clear and visible rules?

Rationale Strong governance can reduce value lost through fragmented decisions.

Foundation 03

Training and Execution

Question Do staff understand the process and its impact on the guest?

Rationale National improvements depend on local execution.

Foundation 04

Customer Capture

Question Can restaurants attract hotel guests and local diners without broad discounts?

Rationale Relevant experiences and clear choices can create repeat demand.

04 / Strategy

Recommended Strategy

Our recommendations formed four workstreams. Revenue and operations created the foundation. Guest engagement and experience supported growth.

Revenue

Revenue Management

Use structured menu reviews, pricing processes, and discount governance.

Operations

Operational Systems

Standardize data, strengthen review cycles, and improve coordination.

Engagement

Guest Spending

Use menu engineering, guided selling, pairings, and targeted offers.

Experience

Guest Retention

Use loyalty, local relevance, signature experiences, and targeted marketing.

We moved from isolated tactics to one commercial playbook. Each idea linked to a business objective and an operating requirement. Each idea also included a measurement plan.

05 / Technological Implementations

Technological Implementations

We evaluated technology based on its ability to improve decisions. The tools needed to organize menu, pricing, labor, and guest data. They also needed to support consistent reviews across locations.

We recommended a phased pricing and review system. The first step was a pilot at selected properties. The pilot would test data flows, local exceptions, and manager training needs. National expansion would follow the pilot.

06 / Staff Training

Staff Training

Written policies were not enough. The training model needed mentorship, practical examples, and person-to-person reinforcement. Passive modules could support the process. They could not replace practice and feedback.

Staff needed specific language and examples for guest interactions. Role-playing helped teams practice these conversations. Guided selling should help diners make confident choices. Employees also needed to understand the menu and the guest’s needs.

01

Demonstration

Use examples, scripts, and manager modeling.

02

Practice

Use role-playing and on-the-job coaching.

03

Reinforcement

Give timely recognition and feedback.

04

Performance Tracking

Monitor adoption and training outcomes.

07 / Implementation Plan

Implementation Plan

The roadmap placed foundational work before guest-facing expansion. Reliable data, governance, and operating practices came first. Loyalty, pricing, and digital experiences followed.

Phase 01

Stabilization

Clarify ownership, strengthen training, and improve data practices.

Phase 02

Standardization

Establish shared reviews, commercial rules, tools, and playbooks.

Phase 03

Growth

Pilot guest concepts, apply lessons, and scale successful ideas.

Each phase included owners, dependencies, training needs, risks, and measures of progress. This kept the roadmap practical and focused on execution.

08 / Scaling Strategy

Scaling Strategy

Every property operates in a different market. Regional demand, restaurant concepts, local costs, physical constraints, guest patterns, and digital adoption can vary. The decision process should remain consistent. This includes data definitions, governance, review questions, and pilot methods.

Local execution should remain flexible. Each property can select the right menu, message, channel, activation, and experience. This balance protects quality and preserves a sense of place.

09 / Key Takeaways

Key Takeaways

Operational Foundations

Guest-facing ideas depend on reliable data, clear ownership, consistent rules, and strong execution. These foundations must come first.

Scalable Strategy

Scaling does not mean repeating the same solution everywhere. The core process should stay consistent. Local execution should adapt to each property.

The project changed how I think about growth. Growth requires more than new customers or higher prices. It also requires the systems and people needed to sustain change.
