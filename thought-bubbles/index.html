01

philanthrocapitalism

Many billionaires in our society can replace Batman in his story with their multinational conglomerate and private foundations. Today, we live in a "Batman Society" where society’s wealthiest elites are characterized as saviors who uplift the poor, empower the marginalized, and advocate for the disenfranchised. But this narrative fails to acknowledge the unequal distribution of power and the exploitative systems and regulations that enable them to maintain their domination over society and exacerbate this power inequality. The essay critiques the rise of philanthropy by multibillionaires where the elite leverage their financial power to shape societal priorities, often reinforcing existing power structures under the guise of benevolence. Through examples, such as the philanthropic activities of figures like Bill Gates and Mark Zuckerberg, it reveals the contradictions and self-serving motivations underlying these acts of generosity, from tax benefits to reputation cleansing.

02

beyond raising awareness

In an age where virality often replaces value, raising awareness has become the go-to strategy for driving social change. From the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge to #BlackoutTuesday and pink ribbon campaigns, awareness movements generate massive participation and funding, but often without depth, context, or long-term impact. This essay critiques the overreliance on performative activism, exposing how many modern campaigns prioritize trendiness and visibility over genuine understanding and measurable change. Through examples across public health, racial justice, and corporate philanthropy, it reveals how well-intentioned awareness efforts can backfire, perpetuate misinformation, or even distract from more urgent causes. Ultimately, the essay challenges the notion that awareness alone is enough, arguing for a more deliberate, informed, and action-oriented approach to social movements.

03

labor shortage in Singapore's fintech industry

Singapore, despite its relatively small population, has solidified its position as a leading fintech hub in Asia. This growth, however, has revealed a critical issue: a shortage of tech talent. This essay investigates how private investments in human capital and government initiatives address labor shortages in Singapore's fintech industry. It highlights the challenges posed by the pandemic, rapid digitalization, and changing skill demands in the industry. By analyzing wage and non-wage factors, the study evaluates the efficacy of measures like wage subsidies, upskilling and reskilling programs, policy for foreign workers and the use of AI and other technological advancements to enhance productivity. The study underscores the limited impact of government programs due to low private sector awareness and misalignment with industry needs, while also exploring alternatives like AI integration and foreign talent.

04

green perception regarding Tesla

Today, more and more businesses regard environmental sustainability as a critical factor to success. This research paper investigates the factors influencing Taiwanese consumers' purchase intentions toward Tesla electric vehicles, specifically examining the relationships between green brand image, green trust, green perceived value, and green purchase intention. Using survey data from 225 respondents and statistical methods like structural equation modeling, the study finds that a positive green brand image significantly impacts both green trust and green perceived value. Furthermore, green perceived value strongly predicts green purchase intention, while the direct effects of green brand image and green trust on purchase intention are less significant. We conclude that building a strong green brand image and fostering green trust, ultimately leading to high green perceived value, are crucial for increasing sales of electric vehicles.
